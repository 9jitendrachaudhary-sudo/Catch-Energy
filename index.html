Why heat recovery

Industry wastes 20–50% of the energy it buys. We help you catch it.

Flue gases, jacket water, condenser circuits, compressed air systems, data hall exhaust — most sites reject enormous quantities of usable heat to atmosphere while paying for gas and electricity next door.

Absorption technology converts that rejected heat directly into chilled water or upgraded heating, with minimal electrical input, near-silent operation and refrigerants with zero ozone depletion potential.

  • Cut electrical cooling costs and CO₂ emissions
  • Improve CHP and district heating scheme economics
  • Meet ESG, SECR and net-zero commitments with real engineering

FAQ

Common questions about absorption technology

Clear answers for engineers, energy managers and project teams evaluating heat-driven cooling and heat recovery.

What is an absorption chiller?

An absorption chiller produces chilled water using heat (hot water, steam or exhaust gas) as the driving energy instead of an electric compressor. It is ideal where waste heat is available and can cut electrical cooling demand dramatically.

What is the difference between a low grade and high grade absorption heat pump?

A low grade absorption heat pump combines driving heat with low-grade waste heat to deliver useful heating at up to about 90 °C with a COP around 1.7. A high grade heat pump (heat transformer) lifts medium-grade waste heat 30–50 °C higher with almost no external energy input.

Do you provide maintenance and emergency support?

When should I choose an absorption chiller instead of an electric chiller?

Choose absorption when you have available waste heat, steam, hot water or CHP exhaust, or when the site is grid-constrained. Absorption converts that heat into cooling with very low electrical input.

Absorption heat pumps

Upgrade low-grade waste heat into useful energy.

Absorption heat pumps recover heat that would otherwise be rejected — from flue gases, cooling circuits or sewage — and lift it to useful temperatures for district heating and process use.

Low grade

Low Grade Heat Pump

Combine a high-temperature driving source with low-grade waste heat to deliver a large flow of medium-temperature heat — COPs around 1.7 mean roughly 1.7 kW of useful heat for every 1 kW of driving heat.

  • Output tempUp to ~90 °C
  • Typical COP1.6 – 1.8
  • Best forDistrict heating, flue gas recovery
High grade

High Grade Heat Pump

Take medium-temperature waste heat and lift a portion of it to a higher temperature — with almost no external energy input. Ideal where process waste heat exists but higher-grade heat is needed.

  • Temperature lift+30 – 50 °C
  • Typical COP≈ 0.45 – 0.5
  • Best forProcess industry upgrades
